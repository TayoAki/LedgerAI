# What bookkeeping Firms pay today for AI bookkeeping software

Researched on 2026-11-14. Every price below was read on the vendor's own website on that date unless it is marked **[Not verified]**. All prices are US dollars per month unless stated otherwise.

This note feeds one decision: the pricing unit, price and trial for Ledger. It does not recommend a price.

## Summary in plain language

- **Charging per Client per month is the norm.** Of the 12 products compared, 9 charge the Firm for each Client it connects. Charging per staff member ("per seat") is used by the practice-management products that have added bookkeeping features (Financial Cents, Client Hub). Charging by number of transactions is rare in the US and was only found as a main unit in a UK product.
- **Published prices fall into two bands.** Tools sold as a workspace that *helps the Firm's own bookkeepers* (chasing Clients for answers, checking the books, tracking the close, sometimes with AI suggestions) cost about **$5 to $25 per Client per month**. Tools sold as an *"AI bookkeeper" that does the categorising and reconciling and leaves the Firm to review* cost about **$50 to $150 per Client per month**.
- **The cheapest reference point is QuickBooks' own maker.** Intuit is giving Firms a free workspace (Intuit Accountant Suite) and has announced a month-end close add-on at $8 per Client per month ($6 above 50 Clients) from 20 January 2027, although Intuit's own pages do not fully agree on this. Its AI-agent version of the close is free while in beta and its later price is not announced.
- **Most vendors publish at least some prices, but the ones most like Ledger do not.** 8 of the 12 publish full prices, 3 publish part of them, 1 publishes none. The products that describe themselves the way Ledger does (AI agents that run the month-end close for a Firm) mostly say "book a demo" (Basis, Truewind, Zeni for firms, TaxGPT). The one published price for that kind of product is Double's "Autoclose": $500 per month for the whole Firm, on top of $10 to $50 per Client.
- **True self-serve sign-up is less common than published prices.** Only about half let a Firm start without talking to anyone. Several that publish prices still route "Get started" to a sales call or demo (Double, Xenett, Client Hub); Botkeeper's buttons open a contact form.
- **Where a free trial exists it is usually 14 days with no card.** Digits offers 30 days. Botkeeper states plainly it has no free trial and offers month-to-month billing instead.
- **Volume discounts and "unlimited staff users" are standard.** Per-Client prices drop sharply as the Client count grows (Botkeeper falls from $149 to $59 between 1 and 25 Clients). Almost every per-Client product lets the whole Firm team log in at no extra charge.
- **Almost all of them work with QuickBooks Online, and most include some way to collect answers or documents from the Client.** The exceptions are the newer products that replace QuickBooks with their own ledger (Digits, Kick).

## How the products were chosen

The starting list came from searching for AI bookkeeping, categorisation, reconciliation and month-end close tools aimed at accounting and bookkeeping practices, and from the "compare us with" lists on the vendors' own sites. Review sites and comparison articles were used only to find names; no price in this note is taken from them unless marked.

A product made the main table if it met all of these:

1. It is sold to Firms that keep books for many Clients (it has a multi-Client workspace, partner pricing or a firm plan), not only to a small business for its own books.
2. It automates part of bookkeeping or month-end close with AI: categorising transactions, reconciling accounts, reviewing the books, or running the close.
3. It is sold in the US market, where QuickBooks Online is the common ledger.

Within that, the 12 were picked to cover each way of charging, so the table shows the spread and not just one corner of the market. A further 9 products that were looked at and set aside are listed after the per-product notes, with their prices where published.

Two things to know about how the pages were read:

- Most pages were downloaded and read directly. Intuit's pages block direct download, so they were read through an automated page reader that was asked to quote word for word, and each was read twice. Treat the Intuit quotes as reliable but worth a second look in a browser before relying on them.
- Dext shows its per-Client rate only through an interactive calculator, which could not be run. Its rate is therefore missing, not hidden.

## Per-product notes

### 1. Botkeeper

- **Price and unit.** Per licence per month, where the pricing page's calculator states "1 license = 1 client". Billed monthly: $149 (1-4 licences), $109 (5-9), $79 (10-24), $59 (25+). Billed annually ("Save 10%"): $134, $98, $71, $53. The page's own calculator applies the $53 rate to all 25 licences, so the tier rate applies to every licence, not just the extra ones.
- **Included at every tier.** AI transaction categorisation, automated bank reconciliation, automated journal entries, "autonomous month-end review with anomaly detection", close tracker, Firm dashboard, password manager, task management, "recurring workflows & client requests", unlimited document storage, self-guided onboarding. The FAQ lists "Portal Manager" and "Documents" among the software features.
- **Sign-up and trial.** FAQ: "We do not offer a free trial. However, we offer month-to-month billing". Monthly plans can be cancelled any time; annual plans are a full-year commitment. The "Sign Up Now" and "Start Now" buttons open a pop-up form and a separate button goes to "meet with sales", so it could not be confirmed that a Firm can pay without contact. Payment by card, Google Pay or US bank transfer.
- **QBO.** Its help centre says "We currently support QuickBooks Online and Xero."
- **Company status, worth knowing.** Trade press reported in February 2026 that Botkeeper was closing **[Not verified: trade press headline only, article not read]**. Xendoo, an online bookkeeping firm, then announced on 3 March 2026 that it had acquired "Botkeeper's AI-powered bookkeeping automation platform, Infinite" and would serve "CPA firms directly on the Botkeeper platform". The pricing page is live and carries a 2026 copyright.

### 2. Double (formerly Keeper)

- **Price and unit.** "Per month/per client": Core $10, Plus $25, Scale $50; Enterprise "Contact sales". A Firm can put different Clients on different tiers. The FAQ says: "You will only be charged per the amount of connected clients at the beginning of your monthly billing cycle. A client is considered 'connected' when you import their QuickBooks Online or Xero ledger files. You can add as many team members, client contacts or unconnected clients as you need, free of charge." Prices are also shown in CAD ($14 / $35 / $70), AUD ($16 / $40 / $80) and GBP (8 / 20 / 40).
- **Included.**
  - Core: two-way ledger sync, custom-branded Client portal, AI bank feeds, task management, Firm-wide reporting and time tracking, vendor W-9 requests, integrated email, "AI Fix this Rec", "Ask Double".
  - Plus adds: one-click AI financial summaries, receipt management.
  - Scale adds: accruals, allocations, AI journal entries, AI bills, AI flux analysis.
  - Enterprise adds: custom contract terms, custom API endpoints, dedicated Slack channel.
- **Firm-wide add-ons.** Autoclose $500 per month ("lets accounting firms hand the work of the close to AI agents": agents fetch statements, categorise, reconcile, then the Firm reviews and signs off). Tax Suite "Included with $200 per month annual commitment". Extra connected team email $10 per month each.
- **Sign-up and trial.** Every plan button goes to "book a demo". FAQ: "Your first step is a personalized demo with our team", followed by a free trial whose length is not stated. A subscription needs at least one connected Client.
- **QBO and portal.** Works with QBO and Xero. The Client portal uses emailed "magic links" in place of passwords.
- **Sources (seen 2026-11-14).**
  - https://doublehq.com/pricing-plans/
  - https://doublehq.com/autoclose/

## The range, summarised

**Pricing unit.**

- Per Client per month: Botkeeper, Double, Xenett, Uncat, Booke AI, Dext, Digits, Kick, and Intuit's Books Close add-on. This is 9 of 12.
- Per staff user per month: Financial Cents and Client Hub. Financial Cents then adds a per-Client charge for its month-end tool.
- Flat fee for the whole Firm: used only as an extra layer (Double Autoclose $500; Intuit Accelerate $149).
- By volume of transactions or documents: not used as the main unit by any of the 12. It appears as a "free below 250 transactions a year" threshold (Digits, Kick), as top-up credits (Dext), and as the main unit only in Briefcase (UK) and AutoEntry.
- Not published: Basis.

Across everything published, the lowest figure is $5 and the highest is $300 per Client per month.

**How many publish prices.** Of the 12: 8 publish full prices (Botkeeper, Double, Xenett, Uncat, Financial Cents, Client Hub, Intuit, Kick). 3 publish part (Booke AI and Digits publish a per-Client list price but not what Firms actually get; Dext publishes through a calculator). 1 publishes nothing (Basis). Among the 9 set aside, 5 publish prices and 4 do not.

**Self-serve.** A Firm can start alone with Uncat, Financial Cents, Kick, Dext (trial), Booke AI ($129 plan) and Intuit. It must talk to someone first with Double, Client Hub, Xenett, Basis, and Digits' Firm programme. Botkeeper is unclear.

**QBO and Client-facing features.** 9 of the 12 work with QBO; Digits and Kick replace it; Basis does not say on the pages read. A Client portal by that name is included in Double, Xenett, Financial Cents, Client Hub and Digits, and Botkeeper lists a "Portal Manager". Uncat, Dext and Booke AI collect answers or documents from the Client without calling it a portal. Intuit offers a Client inbox with file sharing.

## What this means for the decision

The decision is the pricing unit, the price and the trial for Ledger. These are the facts that bear on each. The choice is the owner's.

**Pricing unit.**

- Firms that buy this kind of software are used to paying per Client per month, with the whole team included. A per-Client price for Ledger would need no explaining. A per-seat or per-transaction price would be unusual for an AI bookkeeping product and would need a reason.
- The count that vendors bill on is "Clients connected at the start of the billing month" (Double, Intuit), with part-month charges when a Client is added mid-cycle (Booke AI, Digits). Several let the Firm put different Clients on different plans (Double, Digits, Kick).
- The only published price for "AI agents run the close" is a flat Firm fee on top of per-Client fees (Double: $500 per Firm plus $10 to $50 per Client). So a two-part price (a Firm fee plus a per-Client fee) has one precedent.
- None of the 12 varies the per-Client price by how many transactions the Client has, although Digits and Kick both treat "under 250 transactions a year" as a free or cheaper case. Whether Ledger's cost per Client varies a lot with transaction volume is not known from this research and matters here.

**Price.**

- The two bands are far apart: $5 to $25 for workspaces that assist, $50 to $150 for products sold as doing the work, roughly five to ten times more. Which band Firms place Ledger in will depend on how it is described and what it demonstrably does. As Ledger was described for this research (collecting documents, preparing the close packet, an independent review, a Client portal), it covers ground that Firms today buy as two or three separate products across both bands. That is a reading of the product descriptions, not something a vendor states.
- Intuit sets a low anchor inside QuickBooks itself: a free Firm workspace, $6 to $8 per Client for its close workflow from January 2027, and an AI-agent close with no announced price. Any Firm on QBO will see that price next to Ledger's.
- Every vendor with tiers discounts heavily for volume: Botkeeper's rate at 25+ Clients is 40% of its rate at 1-4; Kick goes from 10% to 50% off at 20 Clients; Intuit drops from $8 to $6 above 50. A single flat rate at all sizes would be the exception.
- The products most similar to Ledger (Basis, Truewind, Zeni for firms) do not publish prices, so there is no public figure for what Firms pay for an agent-run close beyond Double's. Publishing a price for that kind of product would itself be a point of difference.
- This research found what vendors list, not what Firms actually pay after discounts, and not what Ledger costs to run per Client.

**Trial.**

- Where a self-serve trial exists it is 14 days with no card (Xenett, Dext, Financial Cents, Client Hub's assisted trial), or 30 days (Digits).
- The two established products in the "does the work" band do not offer an open trial: Botkeeper has none and offers month-to-month billing instead; Double gives a trial only after a demo.
- Two vendors give the Firm's own books free in place of a time-limited trial (Digits "Free Firm Books", Kick "Free firm account"). Intuit uses a long free beta.
- A month-end close product has a natural cycle of about a month. None of the vendors explains how its trial length relates to that cycle; a 14-day trial may not span one close.
- A trial of Ledger uses real AI work on real Client books. What one trial costs Ledger is not known from this research.

**Commitments and minimums seen.** Dext: 10-Client minimum. Financial Cents: 12-month agreement on every plan, 5-user minimum for monthly billing. Kick: minimum commitment for the 50% tier. Botkeeper: annual plans are a full-year commitment. Double: at least one connected Client. Uncat, Xenett and Booke AI state no minimum.

## Open questions

1. **Dext's per-Client rate.** It is on the page but only inside a calculator. Opening https://dext.com/us/partner/pricing in a browser and setting the Client count would answer it in a minute.
2. **Booke AI's Firm price and Digits' partner-plan prices.** Both are "on request". Only a sales enquiry would reveal them.
3. **What Basis, Truewind, Zeni (for firms) and TaxGPT charge.** These are the closest to Ledger and none publishes. Trade press or a direct enquiry would be the only route; any figure from the press should be treated as unverified.
4. **Intuit's final Books Close price and the Agentic Books Close price.** Intuit's own pages disagree today ($8 / $6 per Client in the help article; "no additional cost" on the pricing page) and the agent version has no post-beta price. Worth re-checking close to 20 January 2027.
5. **Botkeeper's footing.** It closed and was bought within weeks in early 2026. Whether its current list prices will hold, and whether a Firm can really buy without a salesperson, is unclear.
6. **Trial lengths not stated.** Uncat and Double do not give a number of days on their current pages.
7. **Actual prices paid.** List prices only. Negotiated discounts, especially above 50 Clients, are not visible.
8. **What Firms charge their Clients for monthly bookkeeping.** Not researched. It sets the ceiling on what a Firm can afford per Client.
9. **Ledger's own cost per Client per month** (AI usage, QuickBooks connection, hosting) and how much it varies between a small and a busy Client. Not researched. It sets the floor.
10. **How many Clients a typical target Firm has.** The 25-Client illustration is an assumption; the volume tiers vendors use (5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100) suggest most buyers sit below 50, but this was not verified.
11. **Markets outside the US.** Only US prices were compared, apart from Briefcase. Double, Dext and Xenett publish prices in other currencies.
